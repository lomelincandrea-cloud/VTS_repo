How maintenance helps

A system that is mowed, checked and logged on a schedule is easier to defend than one nobody visits. The maintenance tool gives you a dated record you can print.

Maintain a system

Two ways to keep it working. Pick one, or mix them.

A trained crew does it

A maintenance workforce visits on a schedule, does the checks and the hands-on work, and leaves you the record.

  • You always know who is coming, when, and in what vehicle.
  • The crew talks to you first about anything they find.
  • What the crew sees stays with the team and the site partners.
Request maintenance

The workforce model is a proposal. Who hosts it and how it is funded are still open.

Your own people do it

Use the same checklists and plan the crew uses. The tool reminds you what is due and keeps the log.

  • Checklists your irrigators can fill on a phone or on paper.
  • A yearly plan for each system.
  • Records you can print and share if you choose.

How it works

  1. 1Register your systemType, size and watershed. Location stays private.
  2. 2Get a yearly planRoutine visits, a spring visit, a pre-storm visit in August, a January review, and checks after storms.
  3. 3Do the visit or request oneThe Visit Sheet goes to a phone. Anything abnormal becomes a follow-up job.
  4. 4Keep the recordEvery visit lands in the Site History. Fixes land in the Corrective Maintenance Log.

What happens next

  1. We call you to understand the system and agree on access.
  2. A first visit to look at the system with you.
  3. You get a proposed yearly plan and decide what you want.

What we ask before a crew can come

  • Permission from the landowner.
  • A contact on the ranch who knows the crew is coming.
  • Any food safety or biosecurity rules the crew must follow.
